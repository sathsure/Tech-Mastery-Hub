# 🎯 Job Search, Resume & Switching Jobs

## Resume

### ❓ What makes a good tech resume?

A good resume shows **impact**, not just duties, and is easy to scan in 30 seconds.

- **One page** for up to ~7 years of experience; two pages at most after that.
- Order: name and contact (email, phone, LinkedIn, GitHub) → short summary → skills → experience → projects → education.
- Each bullet: **action verb + what you did + result with a number**.
- Match the job's keywords honestly — many companies filter with an applicant tracking system (ATS).
- Plain layout: no photos, tables or text in images, which ATS tools often can't read. Save as PDF.

**Weak:** "Worked on APIs using Spring Boot."
**Strong:** "Built 12 Spring Boot REST APIs for order tracking used by 40,000 daily users; cut p95 latency from 1.8 s to 350 ms with Redis caching."

---

### ❓ How do you write impact bullets if you don't have numbers?

Estimate honestly, or use other measurable results: time saved, errors reduced, users or teams affected, scale of data, or what became possible.

**Examples:**
- "Automated the release checklist with a Jenkins pipeline, cutting each release from about 2 hours to 15 minutes."
- "Wrote integration tests for the payment module; production incidents in that area dropped from monthly to none in 6 months."
- "Mentored 3 freshers; all handled on-call independently within 2 months."

---

### ❓ How should freshers and career-switchers fill a resume?

Replace work experience with **proof you can build things**:

- 2–3 solid projects with a GitHub link and a live demo — one full-stack app, one that uses a real API or data, one in your target area.
- Internships, hackathons, open-source pull requests, certifications that matter for the role (e.g. AWS Cloud Practitioner).
- For switchers: lead with transferable skills (a support engineer's debugging, a tester's automation) and keep older non-tech roles to one line each.

## Job Search

### ❓ What is the best way to find jobs and get interviews?

In order of how well it works in India:

1. **Referrals** — the highest response rate. Ask former colleagues and alumni; send them the job link and your resume so it's easy.
2. **Recruiters on LinkedIn** — keep your profile's headline and skills current; set "Open to work" for recruiters only.
3. **Company career pages and job boards** — Naukri, LinkedIn Jobs, Instahyre, Wellfound (startups), Cutshort.
4. **Communities** — tech meetups, open-source projects, and posting your work publicly.

Apply to roles that match 60–70% of the requirements — job descriptions are wish lists.

---

### ❓ How do you make your LinkedIn profile work for you?

- **Headline:** role + core stack + what you're good at — "Java Backend Developer · Spring Boot, Microservices, AWS · Payments".
- **About:** 3–4 lines on what you build and your best results.
- **Experience:** the same impact bullets as your resume.
- **Skills:** the top 10 keywords recruiters search for your role.
- A professional photo and a custom URL; post or comment occasionally about what you are learning or building.

---

### ❓ How do you prepare for a technical interview in 4–6 weeks?

1. **Week 1:** understand the job — list its stack; refresh core concepts (language, framework, SQL).
2. **Weeks 2–3:** DSA and coding practice — 2–3 problems a day by pattern (arrays and hashing, two pointers, trees, graphs); write code without autocomplete.
3. **Week 4:** system design basics (for 3+ years) and your own projects — be able to draw and defend their architecture.
4. **Week 5:** mock interviews (with friends or tools), behavioral stories using STAR.
5. **Final days:** revise notes, research the company, prepare questions to ask.

Use the tutor's **Interview prep** and **Mock interview** to practise each stack.

## Offers & Switching

### ❓ How do you handle the notice period when switching jobs?

Indian notice periods are usually **30–90 days**. Plan around them:

- Say your notice period honestly in interviews; some companies only hire people who can join within 30 days.
- Ask whether your employer allows early release, adjusting unused leave, or a **notice buyout** (you pay, or the new employer reimburses).
- Don't resign until you have a **written offer**; keep the offer letter, relieving letter and experience letter safe.
- Hand over cleanly — your reputation travels with you.

---

### ❓ How should you handle a counter-offer?

Your current employer may offer more money to keep you. Before accepting, ask why you started looking — if it was the work, growth, team or manager, money rarely fixes it.

- Compare the full offers, not just CTC — use the Atlas's **Pay & Tax → Compare offers**.
- Get any counter-offer in writing.
- Withdraw politely from whichever offer you decline; don't accept an offer you won't take.

---

### ❓ What should you do after receiving an offer letter?

1. **Check the salary breakup:** fixed vs variable, what's inside CTC, PF method, joining bonus terms — see the salary notes.
2. **Check the role:** title, level, location, work mode, reporting manager.
3. **Check the conditions:** notice period at the new company, background verification, any bond or clawback.
4. **Negotiate once, politely,** with data — then accept in writing.
5. **Keep everything** — offer letter, emails, and later your relieving and experience letters.

See also: [What should I check in an offer letter's salary breakup?](./01-Salary-Compensation-India.md)

---

### ❓ How do background verification (BGV) checks work?

After you accept, most companies hire an agency to verify your education, past employment (dates, title, sometimes salary), identity and address, and sometimes criminal records.

- Make sure your resume's dates and titles match your relieving letters exactly.
- Keep payslips, offer letters, relieving and experience letters, and Form 16/130 for every job.
- A mismatch — even an innocent one — can delay or cancel an offer, so explain any gap up front.
